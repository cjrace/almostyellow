# Online Uno

Uno played online at `/games/uno`: an authorised host opens a room, up to ten players join from their own phones, and the server deals, checks every move and keeps each hand private. This page covers the rules engine, the table, the game service and the page. The Cam and Laura score tracker is separate; see [uno.md](uno.md).

## Rules engine (`src/unogame/engine/`)

A pure TypeScript engine with no Firebase, React, clock or randomness of its own. The caller passes `now` and a `shuffle` function with every command, and keeps the full state on the server. Shuffling stays authoritative there.

- 108 cards, each with a stable id (0-107). Seven each, one card per turn. The opening discard is always a number card.
- No stacking. Wild Draw Four can be played at any time. A player can only draw when nothing in their hand can be played (the engine refuses with `must_play`), and may then play or keep that one card.
- With two players Reverse acts as Skip. An action card that empties a hand wins the round and its effect is not applied.
- The first empty hand wins one point. `nextStartingIndex` rotates the starting player between rounds.
- The wild colour is chosen in the same command as the play.
- When the stock runs out, every discard except the top card is reshuffled. Penalties draw what exists. If the stock cannot be refilled and nobody holds a playable card, the round ends as `blocked` with no winner. Cards are never created or lost.

### Uno timing

Playing down to one card with `declareUno` opens no window. Without it, the player is protected until `now + 3000 ms`. After that any other player can catch them, adding two cards once, until the next draw or play by anyone. If the next player acts inside the protected time, the chance to catch is gone.

### Views

`publicView` exposes hand counts, the top card, the colour in force, the turn and any Uno window. `privateView` adds one player's own hand and playable cards. `publicEvent` removes drawn cards from events. Hands and stock order must never leave the server.

### Known limits

There are no turn timers. A player who stops responding holds up the table until the host removes them or ends the round.

## Cards and table components

- `scripts/generate-uno-svgs.mts` writes 54 faces to `public/images/uno`, in the same 5:7 style as the playing cards. Coloured faces spell their colour at the top and bottom and in the image title. The back is the existing Mr Tayto card. Run it again after changing the design.
- `src/unogame/cards.ts` names cards and finds their images. The playing-card API is untouched.
- Components in `src/components/`: `unocard` (images and card buttons), `unohand`, `unoopponents`, `unocolourpicker`, `unoscoreboard` and `unotable`, which composes them. They show a published view and report choices; they never change the game. Styles are in `src/styles/unotable.module.css`.
- The draw pile is greyed out whenever you cannot draw. Its reason is kept as a hidden description and only shown, in the same pop-up as other table alerts, when you try to draw. On a phone held upright the turn heading shares a line with the Room button, and on the lobby and round-over pages the main buttons stay at the bottom of the screen, above the confetti credit.
- `ViewportHeight` publishes the window's measured height as `--app-height` and re-measures after rotating, because installed Android apps can keep a stale `100dvh` after landscape and then scroll in portrait. `cardgame.module.css` prefers it to `100dvh` while it is set.
- Unplayable cards stay focusable and are marked `aria-disabled`, with the reason in their name. Playable cards also stand higher in the hand, and the player to move has a filled pill and the word "Playing", so nothing depends on colour. The pill strip shows everyone, you included, in seat order, with an arrow for the direction of play, and "UNO!" stays on anyone down to one card who has said it. The colour in play has a shape as well as a colour.
- Your hand is always sorted for you (`src/unogame/handsort.ts`): red, yellow, green, blue, then wilds, with each colour in number order and then Skip, Reverse and Draw Two. A card you draw slots into place and keeps a small "New" tag and "just drawn" in its name, so it is still easy to find. Only what is shown is sorted; the server keeps its own order, and the keyboard and screen reader order follows what you see.
- Your hand is an overlapping fan (`src/unogame/handlayout.ts`) that always fits the width it has, so there is no scrolling. Each card keeps its top-left corner showing. On a screen 600 pixels tall or more the hand wraps into balanced rows (up to four) as soon as the strips would be narrower than 40 pixels, so each card is an easy target; otherwise, or beyond four rows, it wraps only when the narrowest strips would not fit. Each row overlaps the one above with its top strip showing. The focused or hovered card comes to the front.
- On a phone held sideways (landscape and 500 pixels tall or less) the table gets as much of the screen as the page can give. `Uno` sets `data-uno-table` on the `<html>` element while the table shows, and `global.css` then hides the site header. The page puts its own back button, a screen-reader-only `Uno` heading, and the header's confetti and light/dark buttons (in the room menu) in their place; all three are hidden in every other layout. One strip runs across the top: back, whose turn it is ("Your turn" on one line), the other players as seats, the info button, Scores and the Room button. Below it the piles are in the middle of the screen, with your buttons in a column on the right at the bottom, in reach of a thumb: Draw a card (filled when nothing can be played, and only in this layout; the draw pile still works), Keep, Say Uno and Catch. The hand runs across the bottom at the full width, with cards 20% of the screen's height wide, about 80 pixels at 402, with the piles kept to about a third of the height. The page, its head, the table and the board are all `display: contents`, so every piece is placed by line on one grid in `unotable.module.css`. The hand, piles and buttons stay on screen without scrolling at 874 by 402, 960 by 448 and 667 by 320 pixels (the hints under the buttons are only read out, and so are the words "Colour in play"). A hand of 50 or more cards and a table of ten players scroll. The other players are two rows at most and scroll inside their box, which becomes a keyboard stop while it scrolls, and which follows the turn. The keyboard visits the back button and the Room button before the table.
- The room code and menu are one button at the top right at every size and in every stage ("Room ABCD"). Its menu holds Copy join link and Leave game, plus End round and End room for the host, and on a phone held sideways Unleash confetti and Light or Dark mode. The "Join link copied" notice goes after three seconds or when the stage changes. The game instructions are only offered at the table. The players are small pills that wrap, not a scrolling row, so the current player is never hidden; "Waiting" is left out and "Playing", "UNO!" and "Uno?" stay. The hint under Say Uno! is only read out. On tall screens the hand’s cards are up to 132 pixels wide.
- In every view, the colour in play is also drawn as a shape on the corner of the discard pile, a soft glow sits behind your hand while it is your turn (still under reduced motion), and Say Uno! only appears when saying it matters (two cards with a playable one, or just after playing down to one), filling in and pulsing three times (not at all with reduced motion). Choosing a colour for a wild card is a dialog.
- Only one thing at a time is bright apart from the turn: the move to make (Say Uno!, Catch, or Draw when nothing can be played) is a filled button, and the seat of someone who can be caught is red and reads "Uno?". The turn itself is a solid orange pill ("Your turn", or "Playing" on a seat). The colour in play tints the table and rings the discard pile, as well as the shape on its corner. In a seat, a few card backs stand for the other player's hand when the seats sit along the top.
- A hand of 12 or more cards gets a "Playable only" button on your turn, which narrows the hand to the cards you can play (its name says "showing the N you can play") and stays one pressed-or-not button, so pressing it again shows every card. It is off again when the turn passes.
- The screen stays awake during a round (`useWakeLock`, which `useGameInProgress` also uses). Phones buzz (`src/haptics.ts`, which respects reduced motion) when your turn starts, when saying Uno starts to matter, and when you have to pick cards up.

## Game service

Uno rooms share the buzzer's room code, join limits, expiry and cleanup (`src/rooms/service.ts`), told apart by a `game` field on the room (`buzzer` when missing). A room holds two to ten Uno players, or twenty for the buzzer, and each game refuses the other's rooms and commands. Sign-in is separate: Uno guests have `uno-` identities, an `unoRoom` token claim and a `uno-guest-<code>` cookie limited to `/api/uno`, so a buzzer sign-in does nothing in an Uno room, or the other way round.

Everything is decided on the server in `src/unogame/service.ts`, which runs the engine inside a Firestore transaction:

- **Lobby.** Joining adds a name to the room's player list. The host starts a round from the lobby (or after a finished round) with at least two players. Starting seats everyone listed, deals, and picks the seat after the previous starter. People who join mid-round wait for the next one.
- **Commands.** `/api/uno` takes `play`, `draw`, `keep`, `catch`, `sayUno` and `leave`, each with a `commandId`. A repeated command id changes nothing. The engine decides whether a move is legal, so most commands do not need the table's version. `catch` does, because it names nobody and could otherwise land on a different player than the one the catcher saw.
- **What is stored.** `gameRooms/{code}` is public to the room: the stage, seats, names, round scores, the public view and the latest events. `roomPrivate/{code}` holds the full round (stock order and every hand) and is never readable by browsers. `roomHands/{code}_{playerId}` is one player's hand and playable cards, readable only by that player while they are in the room. Only hands that changed are rewritten.
- **Leaving.** A seated player can leave, or be removed by the host, during a round. `removePlayer` shuffles their cards back into the draw pile, passes the turn on if it was theirs, and clears any Uno window that was theirs. If one player is left they win and the round ends. Their hand document is deleted. The host can also end a round early with `abandon`, which finishes it with no winner and no score, returning everyone to the lobby.
- **Retention.** A room and its hands expire six hours after it opens. A finished round also writes `roomResults/{code}_{round}`, kept for seven days after the round, so the winner and scores outlive the room.

## Playing online

`/games/uno` (`src/components/uno.tsx`) is the page for playing a round. It mirrors the buzzer: an authorised host creates a room, then everyone joins from a link or a four-character code. The host plays too, so they enter a name like anyone else.

- **Lobby.** Everyone sees the player list. The host removes players and, with the main buttons at the bottom of the page, starts the round (two or more players) or saves to the tracker. Between rounds the page keeps the same order.
- **Winning.** When a round is won while the page is open, everyone gets confetti. The round-over page opens with a centred winner card (a trophy and the winner's name in the Uno colours), then the scores. The host's Play again and Save to tracker (two players only) are at the bottom. There is no modal, so nothing is left open when the next round starts.
- **Pace.** When the turn passes to another player the server records `turnReadyAt` (1.5 seconds on) and refuses `play`, `draw` and `keep` until then ("Wait a moment"). The table dims your hand and draw pile for the same time, so you should rarely hit the refusal. Drawing then playing or keeping the drawn card is the same turn, so it is not delayed; Say Uno and catching are not gated either.
- **End of a round.** When someone wins, every seated player's table stays up for 4 seconds (`FINAL_HOLD_MS`) with the winning card on the pile and "X won the round" as the heading. Then the confetti plays and the round-over page opens. After the hold each device sends a `seen` command, which adds its player to the room's `seen` list. The room records `finishedAt` when a round is won, and `startUnoRound` refuses ("Wait for everyone to see who won") until every seated player still in the room has reported, or 15 seconds (`SEEN_WAIT_MS`) have passed. The host's Play again shows who it is waiting for. Rounds that end without a win (host ended, nobody can move) have no hold or wait.
- **Table feedback.** `src/unogame/alerts.ts` turns the latest events into pop-ups (`UnoAlert`): a dimmed screen with "You pick up 2 cards", "Laura drew a card" and "Laura kept the card they drew" for other players' draws (2 seconds; your own draw is the card turning over), "Laura misses a go", "Laura said Uno!" or a catch. Each alert has its own time on screen: 2.5 seconds for cards and missed goes, 4 for Uno said, 5 for a catch; a batch stays for its longest. Cards fly from your hand to the discard pile and, when you draw, are turned over above the draw pile before joining your hand (`src/components/unoeffects.tsx`); none of this moves under reduced motion. The call-out button only appears once the player's three protected seconds are up.
- **Table.** Seated players get `UnoTable`, fed by two listeners: the room document and their own hand document. Choosing a card, drawing, keeping, saying Uno and catching become commands to `/api/uno`. `catch` also sends the table version it was made against. Someone who joins mid-round waits for the next one.
- **Lost and slow replies.** A command that gets no answer within 2.5 seconds is sent again, with the same command id, while the first copy is still running (`sendUno`). The first answer wins. A copy that fails to connect is resent at once. The server applies a command id at most once, even when both copies arrive together: the receipt is read inside the transaction, and a second copy that loses the race re-reads it and does nothing (`tests/firebase/unoroom.test.ts`). The sign-in token is refreshed as your turn starts, and a refresh that takes more than 5 seconds counts as no answer, so it is resent like any other (`TOKEN_WAIT_MS` in `src/buzzer/client.ts`; the buzzer shares it). The player is only told to look at the table when neither copy gets an answer. Moves are refused while offline or while the listener is serving cached data.
- **Sending…** After a card, draw, keep, Say Uno or catch is tapped, the card images in your hand and on the draw pile are greyed (text keeps its full contrast), and the buttons, hand and draw pile are marked `aria-disabled` and cannot be used again, and a "Sending…" pill (with a turning icon, still under reduced motion) appears above the confetti credit. It reads "Still sending…" after 2 seconds, and is announced politely. It clears when the room changes, when the command fails, or 3 seconds after the server answered (`src/unogame/sending.ts`).
- **Announcements.** `src/unogame/announce.ts` turns the room's public events into sentences for the live region, and adds whose turn it is. It only ever sees public events.
- **After a round.** The room shows who won and the scoreboard. The host can play again, which deals a new round and moves the first turn on one seat.
- **Saving to the tracker.** Between rounds, a host with exactly two players and at least one round won sees **Save to tracker**. They say which player is Cam (the other is Laura) and the date played, and `saveRoomToTracker` adds the room's scores to the Uno tracker as one finished historic game, with the note "Played online (room CODE)". It checks the caller owns the room and that the table has not changed. The game id comes from the room code and expiry (`trackerGameId`), and the room records it as `trackerGameId`, so a room is saved once and the button then reads "Saved to tracker". It adds one game however many rounds were played, so save when the session is over. The tracker's own code (`historicGameEntry`) does the counting, so lifetime totals and statistics match entries made on the history page.
- **Reloading.** The tab remembers the room, and the Firebase sign-in keeps the seat. A host's saved room only gives host controls while the host is signed in.

The first journey in `tests/admin/09_unoplay.spec.ts` cannot know the random deal, so it draws once, then uses the Admin SDK against the emulator to leave one player on a last matching card and plays it. A second journey in the same file has three players, one of whom leaves mid-round, then the host ends the round and plays again. Playing every kind of card is covered by the engine tests. Saving to the tracker is tested in `tests/firebase/unoroom.test.ts` and, with the shared scoreboard, in `tests/admin/07_uno.spec.ts` (desktop Chrome only).
